Summer in the San Fernando Valley puts real demand on air conditioning systems. A little preparation in spring can reduce the chance of mid-season failures and help your equipment run more efficiently.

## Change or Clean the Filter

A restricted filter forces the system to work harder and can lead to frozen coils or reduced cooling. Check your filter monthly during heavy use and replace it according to the manufacturer’s guidance—often every one to three months.

## Clear the Outdoor Unit

Leaves, grass clippings, and debris around the condenser restrict airflow. Keep at least two feet of clearance on all sides and gently rinse the coil fins with a garden hose if they look dirty. Avoid high-pressure sprays that can bend the fins.

## Check the Thermostat

Confirm that the thermostat is set to cool mode and that the setpoint is reasonable. If you have a programmable or smart thermostat, review the schedule so the system is not fighting unnecessary temperature swings.

## Schedule Professional Maintenance

An annual tune-up allows a technician to check refrigerant charge, electrical connections, capacitors, and drain lines before peak demand. Catching a weak capacitor or a clogged drain in March is far better than discovering it on a 105°F afternoon.

## Know the Warning Signs

Unusual noises, weak airflow, short cycling, or higher-than-normal energy bills can indicate developing problems. Addressing them early often costs less than emergency repairs during a heat wave.

If your system is more than a decade old or has needed frequent repairs, it may be worth discussing efficiency and replacement options during a maintenance visit. A properly sized, modern system can improve comfort and reduce operating costs over time.
